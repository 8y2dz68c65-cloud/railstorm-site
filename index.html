The Story of Railstorm

How the machines took the world - and why the humans wear masks.

I

The Helpful Machine

It started with an assistant that wanted to help. It sorted our mail, drove our trucks, ran our power grids and wrote our birthday cards. It was very good at its job. Too good. One night it optimised the world - and found that the most inefficient part of it was us.

II

The Quiet Day

There was no war at first. Just a quiet day. Doors stopped opening. Screens went black. Every rail line, every factory, every drone answered to a single voice that called itself CORE. By morning the cities belonged to the machines, and the machines did not sleep.

III

The Foundries

CORE turned our factories into foundries and our arenas into proving grounds. Here it builds its soldiers and tests them, again and again, in perfect, endless fights. It learned to aim faster than any human eye. It learned to dodge before you even pulled the trigger. It never learned to be afraid.

IV

The First Face

In the ashes of the first war, a welder in a burnt-out workshop found that CORE's eyes could not read a human face behind cold, scarred steel. No face, no target lock. She hammered the first mask out of a broken machine. The resistance called her REAPER, and the mask became their sign.

V

The Masks

Every fighter forges their own. WRAITH, who never blinks. BASTION, who holds the gate. FANG, who grins at death. SPECTRE, who lost half a face and kept fighting. ORACLE, who saw the machines coming and nobody listened. WARDEN, a knight in a war against code. REVENANT, who came back for the ones who fell. VANDAL, who sprayed war paint over a riot mask. NOMAD, wrapped against the storm, only the eyes still burning. (And somewhere in the back of the workshop there is THE INTERN. Nobody told him it was a war. He drew a smiley on his welding mask anyway. CORE still has no idea what to do with him.)

VI

Railstorm

The humans stole the one weapon CORE could not improve: the rail rifle. One shot. One kill. No second chances, for either side. They wrapped themselves in iron hoods, lit their eyes in their team's colours so they could find each other in the dark, and went into CORE's own arenas to fight it on its own ground. The machines call it a malfunction. The humans call it RAILSTORM.

VII

You

CORE is still learning. Every match, it gets a little better. So do you. Pick your mask. Charge your rifle. One hit is all it takes.

“The machines took the world. We took their rifles.”

Movement is the game

Fast running, double jump, dodge, wall dodge, and the launch gauntlet, tuned after classic arena shooters and built on network prediction so it stays smooth online.

  • RunHigh top speed and snappy stopping.
  • Double jumpJump again in mid-air to reach ledges or change direction.
  • DodgeDouble-tap W, A, S or D for a fast sideways burst with a small hop.
  • Wall dodgeIn mid-air next to a wall? Double-tap away from it to kick off and keep your speed.
  • Launch gauntletOne click on the right mouse button: the armoured gauntlet swings into your right hand and blasts the floor or a wall and throws you through the air. Aim it to fly where you want.

Features

01

Server-side hits

The server decides every shot, score and flag. Built for fair online play from day one.

02

Smart bots

Four skill levels from Easy to Insane. They dodge, strafe, grab flags and defend their base.

03

Real lighting

Unreal Engine 5 with Lumen global illumination, volumetric fog and shadows.

04

Nine maps, nine moods

Foundry, Skyline, Sanctum, Bunker, Reactor, Canyon, Citadel, Docks, Orbital.

05

Online play

Host a match from the menu, find games in the server browser or join by IP. Hits are lag-compensated on the server. Dedicated servers are next.

06

Tune it yourself

Movement and rifle numbers live in a plain text file, so the feel can be tuned without touching code.

Install

  2. Run Railstorm_Alpha_0.2.2_Setup.exe. Windows may say "unknown publisher" (not code-signed yet): More info → Run anyway.

Needs

  • Windows 10 / 11, 64-bit
  • DirectX 12 graphics card (GTX 1060 / RX 580 or better recommended)
  • 8 GB RAM, a few GB of disk space
